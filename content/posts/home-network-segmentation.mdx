My home network worked fine as one flat subnet. Everything could talk to everything, which is exactly the problem: a compromised smart plug had the same access to my NAS as my laptop did. The fix isn't complicated in theory. In practice it took a weekend, a managed switch, and more time in the pfSense UI than I expected.

## The layout

Four VLANs, each on its own subnet:

- **VLAN 10, Trusted** — laptops, phones, the stuff I actually manage
- **VLAN 20, IoT** — smart plugs, cameras, anything with a "sign in with your phone" onboarding flow
- **VLAN 30, Guest** — internet only, no access to anything else on the network
- **VLAN 99, Lab** — the servers and containers I break on purpose

Each VLAN gets tagged at the switch, handed to pfSense as a separate interface, and firewalled independently. The rule I cared about most:

```
IoT -> Trusted: block
IoT -> Lab: block
IoT -> Internet: allow
```

IoT devices can phone home to whatever cloud service they need. They cannot reach anything else. That single rule is most of the actual security benefit of this whole project.

## Where it got annoying

Two things:

1. **Chromecast and AirPlay assume flat networks.** Multicast discovery doesn't cross VLAN boundaries by default, so casting from a Trusted-VLAN phone to an IoT-VLAN TV silently failed until I set up an mDNS repeater (`avahi` on a small container) to forward discovery traffic between the two.
2. **Managed switch port configuration is unforgiving.** Get a trunk port's tagged VLANs wrong and you don't get an error, you get a device that just doesn't show up on the network, with no obvious reason why. I ended up keeping a spreadsheet of every port and its VLAN assignment, which felt like overkill until it saved me twenty minutes of guessing on day two.

## Was it worth it

For a home network with eight IoT devices, honestly, probably not from a pure risk-reduction standpoint. But that undersells the actual point: I understand VLAN tagging, trunk ports, and inter-VLAN firewall rules in a way that reading about them never got me to. Building the thing you're trying to learn beats reading about the thing you're trying to learn, at least for this stuff.
